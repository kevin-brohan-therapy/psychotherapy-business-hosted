Process

Finding a psychotherapist can be a daunting process, especially if you have not spoken with one before. It can take some time to find a therapist who feels like a good fit, as each person is different. The following sections explain my process and what you can expect at each stage.

Initial Contact

"Reaching Out"

This is the first step, via the contact form on this website. You can briefly share what is bringing you to therapy, or simply say that you would like to talk. I will respond within a couple of days and find a mutually agreeable time for an introductory call.

Introductory Call

"A short, informal conversation"

For the introductory call, I will contact you for a phone conversation of around 15 minutes. This is a chance for us to connect briefly, answer any initial questions, and get a feel for whether working together might be a good fit. There is no pressure or commitment.

If we decide to work together, I will send you the welcome documents, which contain a brief questionnaire, working agreement, mutual expectations and payment information. We will then agree on a regular appointment time, either online or face-to-face. The initial frequency of appointments will also be agreed at this point.

Assessment

"Beginning to get a sense of things"

We will then enter a period of assessment, which normally lasts for four to six appointments. This is a longer conversation in which we explore what has brought you to therapy, what changes you would like to make in your life, and how we might work together towards those goals.

Sessions during this period are likely to focus on building a therapeutic relationship while developing a clearer understanding of your current situation and the experiences that have shaped it.

Ongoing Therapy

"The main therapeutic work"

Regular sessions begin and we enter an ongoing therapeutic contract. We work together at a pace that feels manageable and meaningful, exploring thoughts, feelings, behaviours, patterns and possibilities. This stage is collaborative and flexible, with a shared focus on the changes we have agreed to work towards.

Closing

"Ending well"

When the time is right, we will plan for an ending together. This might happen naturally over time or become a more structured piece of work. The aim is to reflect on what has changed, what has been learned and what comes next.

People

Kevin Brohan

You may be coming to therapy with a specific difficulty, or with a broader sense of being held back by painful feelings, unhelpful thoughts or difficult patterns of behaviour. Even when life looks fine from the outside, it may feel very different from within.

Common questions at the start of therapy

  • Do your relationships follow repeating patterns?
  • Do you keep finding yourself in unpleasant situations?
  • Are you struggling to cope with adverse events?
  • Do you struggle to control your behaviour?
  • Do you experience sudden states of anger or fear?
  • Do you struggle to contain your feelings?
  • Do you wish you felt differently about situations?
  • Do you struggle with your sexual or gender identity?
  • Are you troubled by thoughts, dreams or fantasies, whether sexual or non-sexual?
  • Do you find yourself being pulled back into difficult experiences from your past?
  • Are you struggling to process feelings from a toxic relationship?
  • Do you worry that you might be bringing toxicity into your relationships?
  • Do you try hard, but never seem to succeed?
  • Are you always in a rush? Are things ever good enough? Are you constantly let down by other people?
  • Do you find yourself living old feelings?
  • Do you find yourself putting other people first?
  • Do you find yourself trying to hold things together, yet feel that no one really sees or knows you?
  • Are you successful on paper, but unable to achieve intimate relationships?
  • Does something about your life feel intolerable?
  • Are you coping with grief or illness?
  • Have you recently received bad news?

Talking to friends or family can be helpful, but sometimes these relationships are part of what maintains the difficulty, or they may not offer the safety or depth you need to examine what is happening. Therapy offers a confidential and impartial relationship in which there is time to explore your experience more fully.

I offer a free initial consultation online or by phone. You are very welcome to get in touch with a question or to arrange a time for us to speak.

I believe that life involves pursuing vitality while maintaining our capacity for introspection and curiosity. Together, we will form a therapeutic alliance focused on understanding what is happening and working towards meaningful and lasting change.

Before beginning my psychotherapy training, I worked in medical and scientific software and carried out academic research in psychology, robotics and artificial intelligence.

I am a Trainee Psychotherapist, a student member of the BACP and a Transactional Analysis Psychotherapy Trainee with UKATA. I work under regular clinical supervision and am also recognised by IARTT to practise the Rewind trauma technique.
